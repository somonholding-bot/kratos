# Human mind and body science (Sol)

Reference for Aion Mind content. Each claim carries an evidence tag: [strong] replicated or meta-analytic, [moderate], [weak/contested], [speculative]. Sources were located via search on 4 Nov 2026; most were checked through abstracts and record pages, not always full text. Re-check the primary paper before quoting numbers on camera.

## How to use this skill
1. **Cite.** Name the source (author, year, journal) in the script notes. If no source, do not state it as fact.
2. **Tag evidence.** Use the four tags above internally; in narration translate them ("well replicated", "early", "debated", "a hypothesis").
3. **State uncertainty.** Prefer "studies suggest" over "science proves". Mention sample size or small effects when the story depends on them.
4. **No medical advice.** Describe research; never prescribe doses, protocols, stimulation devices or drug use. Point viewers to a clinician for sleep, mental-health or medication questions.
5. **No claim of controlling minds.** Never promise to rewire, hack or control viewers' (or anyone's) mind, dreams or health. Persuasion tools are described, not weaponised.

## 1. Attention, perception, memory, emotion, decision-making, biases
**Solid**
- Many classic psychology findings replicate poorly: a 100-study project found replication effects about half the original size and 36% significant vs 97% originally. [strong] https://acawiki.org/Estimating_the_reproducibility_of_psychological_science (paper: Open Science Collaboration 2015, Science, doi 10.1126/science.aac4716)
- Learning styles: a major review found virtually no evidence that matching teaching to a preferred "style" improves learning (the meshing hypothesis). [strong] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26162104/
- Left/right-brain personality: imaging of 1,011 people found lateralisation is local, not a whole-brain "left-brained" or "right-brained" type; hemispheric specialisation (e.g. language) is real. [moderate-strong] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23967180/
- Brain-training: people improve on trained tasks, but broad transfer to everyday cognition is not shown. [strong] https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/brain-training.html

**Contested/hype**
- Ego depletion ("willpower as a battery"): a 23-lab preregistered replication found an effect near zero (d = 0.04) versus earlier meta-analytic d = 0.62. Treat as failed or at best very small. [weak/contested] https://mro.massey.ac.nz/items/4a1951d7-6022-4d0e-bbb6-fc336ecd4792/full
- The "10% of the brain" myth: no source was opened in this research; simply do not use it.
- Cognitive-bias lists are descriptive; individual bias effects vary in size and some (priming, ego depletion) have not held up. [moderate]

**Use in content**
- A strong storytelling angle: "the science that failed to replicate" is itself a credible hook.
- Say "people often..." not "your brain always...". Give the bias a plain example, flag if the original study was small.

## 2. Sleep, dreaming and lucid dreaming
**Solid**
- Adults: a joint AASM/SRS consensus recommends 7 or more hours of sleep regularly. [moderate: expert consensus] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25979105/
- Lucid dreaming is verifiable: Voss et al. (2009) reported EEG differing from both waking and ordinary REM (a "hybrid state", frontal differences), n = 6. [moderate] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19750924/
- Two-way communication: in a multi-lab study (36 participants), people in verified REM answered simple questions with eye or facial signals. Proof of concept; accuracy was limited. [moderate] https://hal.sorbonne-universite.fr/hal-03154112 (Konkoly et al. 2021, Current Biology)
- Reviews: lucid-dream EEG and imaging studies are mostly underpowered; prefrontal and parietal regions are implicated. [moderate] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30880167/ (Baird, Mota-Rolim, Dresler 2019)

**Contested/hype**
- Induction: a systematic review of 35 studies concluded that no technique reliably and consistently induces lucid dreams, though some are promising. [moderate] https://www.pubmed.gov/22841958/ (Stumbrys et al. 2012; note the search listing for this paper pointed to this PubMed record, verify the ID before citing)
- Combined reality testing, wake-back-to-bed and MILD gave about 17% success in a one-week Australian study (Aspy et al. 2017, Dreaming; figure from press coverage, full paper not opened). [moderate] https://www.sci.news/othersciences/psychology/lucid-dreaming-05347.html
- tACS at 40 Hz triggered lucid dreams in roughly two-thirds of a small sample (Voss et al. 2014); replication still needed, and it is a lab method, not a home hack. [weak/contested] https://www.discovermagazine.com/mind/electrical-brain-stimulation-can-trigger-lucid-dreams
- Dream-interpretation claims ("this symbol means X") and "dreams predict the future": no evidence base was found in this research. [speculative]

**Use in content**
- Present lucid dreaming as real, measurable and modestly inducible; be honest that results vary.
- Never promise a viewer they will become lucid in a given number of nights. Do not recommend sleep deprivation or supplements to induce dreams.

## 3. Consciousness science
**Solid (about the field, not the answer)**
- Main families: global workspace (conscious content is broadcast across a widespread network; Baars, Dehaene), integrated information theory (consciousness tied to a system's integrated cause-effect structure; Tononi), higher-order theories, and predictive processing (perception as brain-generated prediction constrained by input; Seth's "controlled hallucination"). [descriptive] https://www.nature.com/articles/s41583-022-00587-4.pdf ; https://loc.closertotruth.com/theory/seth-s-beast-machine-theory
- The Cogitate adversarial collaboration (Nature, 2025) found data matching some predictions of both global workspace and IIT while substantially challenging key tenets of each; no winner. [moderate] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12137136 ; https://quantamagazine.org/what-a-contest-of-consciousness-theories-really-proved-20230824

**Contested/hype**
- IIT: a 2021 letter from 124 experts called it unfalsifiable pseudoscience; supporters dispute this. [weak/contested] https://www.techno-science.net/en/news/how-does-neuroscience-explain-consciousness-25345 (secondary source)
- "Consciousness is quantum" or "the universe is conscious": no empirical consensus. [speculative]
- Whether AI could be conscious depends on the theory (a workspace architecture could run on other hardware; Seth argues biology may matter). [speculative]

**Use in content**
- Narrate it as an open scientific contest. Say "leading theories disagree" and name them. Avoid declaring that science has found the seat of the soul or the "hard problem" solved.

## 4. Meditation, mindfulness, altered states
**Solid**
- Meta-analysis of 47 trials: mindfulness programs give small-to-moderate reductions in anxiety, depression and pain in some clinical groups; low evidence for stress, mood, attention, sleep, and no proof they beat active treatments (drugs, exercise, other therapy). [moderate-strong] https://stafforini.com/works/goyal-2014-meditation-programs-for/ (Goyal et al. 2014, JAMA Internal Medicine)
- Adverse effects exist: in 96 mindfulness-program participants, 83% reported at least one meditation-related side effect, 58% negative-valence, 6% lasting impairment over a month. Single-sample study. [moderate] https://www.brown.edu/news/2021-05-18/adverse-effects (Britton et al. 2021, Clinical Psychological Science)

**Contested/hype**
- "Mind the Hype" (Van Dam et al. 2018) criticises overselling: weak measurement, poor controls, harms underreported. [moderate] https://scholars.mssm.edu/en/publications/mind-the-hype-a-critical-evaluation-and-prescriptive-agenda-for-r-2/
- Psychedelics: promising but early. Trial blinding is often poor and expectancy may inflate effects; the FDA declined to approve MDMA-assisted therapy in Aug 2024 citing data limitations, a decision disputed by the sponsor. [weak/contested] https://www.psychiatrist.com/jcp/psychopharmacology/control-conditions-randomized-trials-psychedelics-acttion-systematic-review/ ; https://www.kpbs.org/news/health/2024/08/09/fda-gives-thumbs-down-to-mdma-for-now-demanding-further-research
- "Enlightenment" or brain-wave claims sold by apps: no source verified. [speculative]

**Use in content**
- Frame meditation as a skill with modest average benefits and possible difficulty. Add a safety line for people with trauma or psychiatric history. Do not encourage psychedelic use or illegal acquisition; describe research and its limits only.

## 5. Biohacking, longevity, neurotechnology
**Solid (basics)**
- Sleep: see section 2 (7+ hours for adults). [moderate] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25979105/
- Exercise: US guidelines for adults are 150 to 300 minutes of moderate (or 75 to 150 vigorous) aerobic activity weekly plus muscle strengthening on 2 or more days. [strong: guideline] https://www.qxmd.com/r/30418471
- Light, nutrition basics, HRV: no source was opened for these in this research. Do not state specifics; if needed, run a separate sourced check.

**Contested/hype**
- Neurofeedback for ADHD: effects appear on unblinded raters but not on probably-blinded ratings or sham-controlled trials (13 trials, 520 children). [moderate] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27238063/ (Cortese et al. 2016)
- tDCS: a review found a reliable effect on only one of 30 physiological measures in healthy adults; a cognitive meta-analysis found no significant single-session effect on cognitive tasks, though both reviews have limits and later work is more nuanced. [weak/contested] https://www.pubmed.gov/26143023/ ; https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/trouble-tdcs-electrical-brain-stimulation-may-not-work-after-all
- Consumer brain-training and "focus" devices: broad benefits not shown (section 1). [strong]
- Supplements, longevity stacks, TMS at home: no source verified in this research. [speculative] Treat as unproven.

**Use in content**
- "Boring basics beat gadgets" is both accurate and a good hook. Include: "This is not medical advice; talk to a clinician." Never recommend stimulation devices, doses or fasting protocols.

## 6. Audiences, persuasion and ethical storytelling
**Solid**
- Narrative transportation: a meta-analysis of 132 effect sizes (76 articles) found stories with realistic detail, identifiable characters and easy mental imagery transport audiences most. [moderate] https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/6755/ (van Laer et al. 2014, Journal of Consumer Research)

**Contested/hype**
- Nudges: Mertens et al. (2022) reported d = 0.45 across 200+ studies; bias-corrected re-analyses suggest near-zero to modest average effects, with real heterogeneity. [weak/contested] https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9351519/ ; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9351501/
- Cialdini's principles (reciprocity, commitment, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity) are widely used, but the searches here found no meta-analytic evidence per principle. Present them as a useful heuristic, not laws. [moderate/unverified]

**Use in content**
- Use story craft to engage, never to deceive: no fake scarcity, no invented testimonials, no fear manipulation, disclose sponsors. Persuade by clarity and relevance, and let viewers opt out.

## Claims to avoid
- "We only use 10% of our brain" (myth); "left-brained vs right-brained people"; "visual/auditory learners learn better when taught that way."
- "Willpower is a limited fuel" as settled fact; "brain-training games make you smarter."
- "Lucid dreaming can be learned in X nights", or that it heals trauma or predicts the future.
- "Science has explained / proven consciousness", "quantum consciousness proven", "IIT/GWT won".
- "Meditation cures anxiety/depression" or "is harmless"; "psychedelics are proven medicines."
- "Nudges always work", "this principle compels people to obey."
- Any dosage, supplement, device, fasting or stimulation advice; any promise of mind control or rewiring.
- Any statistic or study without a source you have opened.
