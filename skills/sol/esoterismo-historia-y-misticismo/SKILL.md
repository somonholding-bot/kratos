# Esotericism, mysticism and parapsychology: honest history (Sol)

Aion Mind presents esotericism as culture, symbol and psychology. It never presents it as proven supernatural power. Tags used below: [documented history], [scholarly consensus], [traditional belief - not demonstrated], [modern invention], [contested]. Sources were opened on 4-nov-2026. Most are Wikipedia pages used as a starting point; before publishing a hard claim, confirm it in the academic works named (Hanegraaff, Faivre, Yates, Scholem, Dobbs, Hyman, Utts).

## How to use this skill
1. Separate history from belief. Say "texts claim" or "practitioners believed", never "it works".
2. Tag the origin of every symbol or text: ancient, medieval, Renaissance, or modern (19th-21st c.).
3. Never present supernatural claims (prediction, telepathy, magical effects) as fact. Where tests exist, state the result.
4. Respect living religions (Judaism, Islam, Buddhism, Aboriginal peoples). Describe practice with care, avoid mockery and avoid flattening them into "mystic wisdom".
5. Cite. Name the author, date and source in scripts and descriptions. If a claim has no source, drop it.

## 1. Hermetism
- Corpus Hermeticum: Greek treatises dated c. 100-300 CE [scholarly consensus]. Casaubon (1614) showed from the Greek that they are not pre-Platonic, ending the "Egyptian ancient wisdom" claim [documented history]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpus_Hermeticum
- Ficino translated them for Cosimo de' Medici (Florence, 1460s); his Latin edition is dated 1471 [documented history]. His *De vita libri tres* (1489) mixes medicine, astrology and Neoplatonism; he was accused of heresy and acquitted [documented history]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsilio_Ficino
- Emerald Tablet: oldest known version is an appendix to the Arabic *Kitab Sirr al-Khaliqa* (late 8th-early 9th c.), which presents itself as by "Apollonius of Tyana" (a pseudepigraph). Latin translations came in the 12th c. A Greek or Syriac original is possible but no manuscript survives [documented history; origin of any earlier source contested]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerald_Tablet
- Kybalion: published 1908 by the Yogi Publication Society as by "Three Initiates", most likely William Walker Atkinson; scholars place it in New Thought, not ancient Hermetism [modern invention; scholarly consensus]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Kybalion
- Frame: Antoine Faivre's four intrinsic traits for "Western esotericism" are correspondences, living nature, imagination and mediation, and transmutation. Hanegraaff has criticised this model as "reasoning by prototype" [contested definition]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_esotericism

## 2. Alchemy
- Origins: attested in pseudepigraphic texts from Greco-Roman Egypt in the first centuries CE; Zosimos of Panopolis (c. 300) is the earliest historically attested author [documented history]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alchemy
- Islamic alchemy: works attributed to Jabir ibn Hayyan (late 9th-early 10th c.) formalised the hot/cold/dry/moist analysis. The "Jabirian corpus" is attributed to one man but its authorship is debated [contested]. Same source.
- Newton: wrote or transcribed about a million words on alchemy; the papers were sold at Sotheby's in 1936 and Keynes bought many [documented history]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Newton%27s_occult_studies
- Jung: *Psychology and Alchemy* (1944). He read alchemical operations as projection of the unconscious onto matter and as a model of individuation. This is a psychological interpretation, not a historical account of what alchemists believed [contested; use as "Jung's reading"]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology_and_Alchemy
- Safe framing: nigredo/albedo/rubedo (black, white, red stages of the Work) are symbolic stages in alchemical texts; the sources checked confirm rubedo as the sign of success and nigredo as Jung's start-of-the-Work stage. Do not claim a single ancient fixed sequence. [contested]

## 3. Kabbalah
- Sefer Yetzirah: short text, dated roughly 200-600 CE [contested range]. Zohar: likely written by Moses de Leon in the late 13th c., though tradition ascribes it to Shimon bar Yochai [scholarly consensus vs traditional belief]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabbalah
- Sefirot: ten divine emanations or attributes, not ten gods. Tree-of-life diagram: first printed in 1516 on the Latin Gikatilla edition (*Porta Lucis*); Kircher's 1652-54 version is the basis for Golden Dawn usage [documented history]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_of_life_(Kabbalah)
- Distinction: Jewish Kabbalah is a Jewish mystical tradition. Christian Kabbalah (Renaissance) and Hermetic Qabalah (19th c. occult) developed independently of it and mix in tarot, astrology and magic [scholarly consensus]. Say "Qabalah" for the occult version, "Kabbalah" for the Jewish one. Same Kabbalah source.

## 4. Ceremonial magic, Thelema, Theosophy
- Agrippa: *De occulta philosophia* (Book 1 Paris 1531, Books 2-3 Cologne 1533; early draft 1510). The *Fourth Book* is not believed to be his [documented history]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Cornelius_Agrippa
- Dee and Kelley: Enochian recorded 1583-84. Linguist Donald Laycock found it English-like, phonologically English, with about 250 words [scholarly consensus]. Origin "from angels" [traditional belief - not demonstrated]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enochian
- Golden Dawn: founded 1887-88 by Westcott, Mathers and Woodman; "Cipher Manuscripts" and the Anna Sprengel contact are Westcott's claims; schism 1900-01; influenced Wicca and Thelema [documented history; Sprengel story contested]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermetic_Order_of_the_Golden_Dawn
- Crowley (1875-1947): *Book of the Law* (1904) which he said was dictated by Aiwass; Abbey of Thelema in Cefalù (1920-23), expelled by Mussolini's government; heavy drug use; press called him "the wickedest man in the world"; some biographers say he worked for British intelligence [contested]. Controversies must be mentioned; avoid glamourising. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleister_Crowley
- Theosophy: Theosophical Society founded 1875 (Blavatsky, Olcott, Judge); *Isis Unveiled*, *The Secret Doctrine*; shaped New Age and artists such as Kandinsky [documented history]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theosophy

## 5. Comparative religion and mysticism
- Gnosticism: modern umbrella term; the 1945 Nag Hammadi find added primary texts; since the 1990s (Williams 1996, King 2005) its validity as a single category is contested [contested]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnosticism
- Sufism: Islamic mystical discipline, mostly Sunni, organised in orders (tariqa) from the 12th-14th c.; "universal mysticism opposed to Islam" is a 18th-19th c. European framing [scholarly consensus]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism
- Eliade: sacred/profane, eternal return, axis mundi. His comparative method is criticised for overgeneralising, and his 1930s support for Romania's Iron Guard is documented. Cite with caution [contested]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mircea_Eliade
- Christian mysticism, Vedanta, Buddhist and Taoist meditation, shamanism: not verified for this version. Before writing about them, open a source (Britannica or SEP) and do not rely on this file. [source needed]

## 6. Moon, planets, astrology, tarot
- Planetary hours and weekday names come from Hellenistic astrology (possibly Babylonian roots); the "Chaldean order" label is modern. The *Key of Solomon* (16th c.) assigns magic to planetary hours [documented history]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_hours
- Astrology: Carlson's 1985 double-blind test in *Nature* with 28 astrologers found chance-level matching; a 40-study meta-analysis found no significant results once clues were removed. Scientists classify it as pseudoscience [scholarly consensus]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrology_and_science
- Tarot: playing cards with trumps (tarocchi) in 1440-50 northern Italy; divinatory use began in 1780s France (Court de Gébelin's unsupported claims 1781, Etteilla c. 1789); Rider-Waite-Smith 1909 [documented history]. "Ancient Egyptian origin" is a [modern invention]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarot

## 7. Dream traditions
- Artemidorus, *Oneirocritica* (2nd c. CE): earliest surviving Greek dream-interpretation manual; meaning depends on the dreamer's circumstances [documented history]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oneirocritica
- Tibetan dream yoga (milam): one of the Six Yogas of Naropa; trains lucidity in dreams [traditional practice; religious claims not demonstrated]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dream_yoga
- Lucid dreaming: Van Eeden coined the term in 1913; Hearne/Worsley (1975) and LaBerge (1980s) verified it with eye signals during REM sleep [documented history]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucid_dream
- Dreamtime: term popularised by Spencer and Gillen (1896-1904); Aboriginal groups differ, and many reject "dream" as implying unreality. Describe with caution, name the specific people, and do not use it as a generic "mystic" brand [contested translation]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreamtime

## 8. Parapsychology and declassified programmes
- Rhine (Duke, from 1927; *Extra-Sensory Perception*, 1934): critics found sensory leakage, poor records, statistical problems and failed replications [documented history; results not accepted]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Banks_Rhine
- Ganzfeld: Honorton 1985 vs Hyman; Milton & Wiseman 1999 found no effect; Storm 2010 reported 32.2% hits against 25% chance; independent replication not achieved [contested by proponents; scholarly consensus: no validated evidence]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganzfeld_experiment
- Bem 2011, "Feeling the Future" (*JPSP*): nine experiments claiming precognition; Ritchie, French and Wiseman failed to replicate (2012); the episode helped trigger the replication-crisis reforms [documented history]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daryl_Bem
- Stargate: SRI work from 1972; Army/DIA programmes (Grill Flame, Sun Streak, Stargate); 1995 AIR review with Utts (argued a statistical effect) and Hyman (argued it was not proven and had no replication); the CIA ended and declassified it in 1995 citing no documented intelligence value; records published in CIA CREST in January 2017 [documented history]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stargate_Project_(U.S._Army_unit) . Shows: the US government tried psychic spying and found no actionable intelligence. Does not show: that remote viewing works. For primary documents, search "Stargate" in the CIA FOIA Reading Room (not opened here).
- Remote viewing: mainstream science calls it pseudoscience; Marks and Kammann found cues in the SRI transcripts [scholarly consensus]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_viewing
- MK-Ultra: began 1953 under Allen Dulles/Sidney Gottlieb; halted 1972; files ordered destroyed 1973; revealed by the Church Committee and Rockefeller Commission (1975); about 20,000 surviving documents found through a FOIA request in 1977; Church Committee said consent was not obtained [documented history]. Shows: unethical drug experiments on people. Does not show: functioning mind control. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_MKUltra

## Claims to avoid
- "The Emerald Tablet is from Hermes Trismegistus / ancient Egypt." Say: earliest known Arabic text, 8th-9th c.
- "The Kybalion is ancient Hermetic wisdom." It is a 1908 New Thought book.
- "Tarot is ancient Egyptian" or "the moon/planets steer your day." No predictive evidence exists; astrology fails controlled tests.
- "The CIA proved remote viewing" or "MK-Ultra proved mind control." Both programmes are documented; neither showed a working power.
- "Bem or Rhine proved ESP." Not replicated.
- "The Flower of Life is 10,000 years old" or "runes were oracles." Unsupported.
- "Dreamtime" as a generic mystical term, and any tarot, astrology or healing advice presented as medical or financial guidance.
