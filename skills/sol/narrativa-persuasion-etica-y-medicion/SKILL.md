# Narrative, ethical persuasion and measurement (Sol)

Companion to `direccion-creativa` (hooks, mentalism, symbols). This file covers structure, persuasion ethics, measurement and learning. Evidence tags: **[strong]**, **[moderate]**, **[craft convention]** (useful working practice, not empirical proof), **[weak/contested]**. Sources are pages that were opened on 4-Nov-2026; items with no URL are craft convention from general practice and must not be presented as research.

## How to use this skill
1. Structures are **lenses, not laws**. Pick one to diagnose a draft; never force a story into a template [craft convention].
2. Every number you state to the owner carries a **range and a confidence level** (section 6). No bare predictions.
3. One result never proves anything. Log it, do not conclude from it (section 5).
4. Persuade only with what the viewer can check. If a tactic stops working once it is explained to the viewer, do not use it (section 4).
5. After every publication, fill the learning-loop entry (section 7) before planning the next video.

## 1. Storytelling and screenwriting craft
- **Aristotle, Poetics**: plot (mythos) is the arrangement of events and the first of six parts of tragedy; it should be one coherent action; reversal (peripeteia) and recognition (anagnorisis) work best together; events follow from probability or necessity, so even surprises feel inevitable afterwards. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poetics_(Aristotle) [strong as canon, craft convention as method].
- **Three acts, McKee (Story), Truby (Anatomy of Story, 22 steps)**: guides that teach structure. McKee's book is widely used but criticised as dogmatic (Vanity Fair 2009, New Yorker 2023, film scholar Barry Langford, as reported) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_McKee. Truby wrote 22 steps as an alternative to the mechanical three-act "paradigm", published 2007 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Truby. [craft convention]
- **Save the Cat** (Snyder, 2005): 15 beats from Opening Image to Final Image; critics (Slate, Suderman) say it hardened into a formula and that hit films use the beats in different order and proportion. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_the_Cat. [craft convention]
- **Hero's journey**: Campbell (1949) proposed a 17-stage monomyth; Vogler condensed it to 12 stages for Hollywood. Academic criticism: selection bias in the myths chosen (Toelken), folklorists' rejection of universal archetypes (Dundes), too abstract and ethnocentric (Crespi), gender-centred (Murdock and others). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monomyth. Use it as a lens for personal-transformation arcs, never claim "all myths follow it". [craft convention; universality claim = weak/contested]
- **Dan Harmon Story Circle**: eight steps (comfort zone, want, unfamiliar situation, adapt, get it, pay a heavy price, return, changed), derived by simplifying Campbell. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Harmon. Good for 8-10 minute episodes and Shorts arcs. [craft convention]
- **ABT (And, But, Therefore)**: Randy Olson's sentence template: set context (And), introduce conflict (But), resolve or advance (Therefore); contrasted with AAA fact-stacking. Olson is a former marine biologist turned filmmaker. Sources: https://www.keyreporter.org/book-reviews/2016/houston-we-have-a-narrative/ and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Olson (the Wikipedia page only lists his books). Use ABT as a quick check: if two beats connect with "and then" instead of "but/therefore", the script is a list. [craft convention]
- **Kishotenketsu**: four parts (introduction, development, twist, conclusion), used in Chinese, Korean, Japanese and Vietnamese storytelling; the twist is not necessarily a conflict. Its origin is uncertain, and "no conflict" is a popular description, not a rule. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kish%C5%8Dtenketsu. Useful for contemplative episodes (a symbol, its development, an unexpected angle, a quiet closing). [craft convention]

## 2. Documentary, explainer and YouTube-native structure
- **Essay film**: develops a theme or idea rather than a plot, blending footage with the maker's reflective voice (Marker, Varda, Welles, Morris). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essay_film. Fits Aion Mind's "one idea, many images" format. [craft convention]
- **Cold open, chapters**: start inside the question or striking scene, then title; split into 3-5 chapters, each ending with a mini-teaser (see `direccion-creativa` for open loops). [craft convention]
- **Hook, retention, payoff, re-hooks**: promise in 0-20 s, make a new micro-promise about every chapter, pay off what the title promised. YouTube's own guidance: the "Intro" key moment is the share still watching after 30 s, and a high value suggests the opening matched title and thumbnail. Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415. The structure itself is [craft convention]; the title-opening match is [moderate, platform guidance].
- **Pacing in words per minute**: audiobook narration is about 150-160 wpm; comfortable slide presentation about 100-125 wpm (cited by the page to Williams 1998 and Wong 2014); auctioneers about 250 wpm. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Words_per_minute [moderate]. Working rule for narration: 140-160 wpm, slower on symbols and the final beat. A 10 min video is roughly 1,400-1,600 words. [craft convention]
- **Visual storytelling for 2D animation**: shot scale (wide for place, medium for action, close-up for emotion or a key symbol), one focal subject per frame, motion direction (left-to-right reads as progress), and a **colour script**: a strip of small colour frames, one per sequence, planning emotional colour over time (a Pixar practice). No source was opened for colour scripts; treat as [craft convention]. For the evidence on colour, rely on `direccion-creativa` (contrast is the only evidenced principle).

## 3. Writing nonfiction, popular-science and esoteric-history books
- **Structure**: one thesis sentence; a chapter map where each chapter answers one question and hands a new question to the next; use ABT at chapter level; end each chapter with a payoff plus a hook. [craft convention]
- **Research notes**: keep one source log per claim (author, edition, page, URL, date accessed, quote under 15 words, your paraphrase, reliability tag). Separate primary sources (the ancient text, the translation) from secondary interpretation. [craft convention]
- **Citation practice**: cite the specific work and translation, not "ancient wisdom says"; say when a meaning is modern (as `direccion-creativa` does). [craft convention]
- **Proposal**: premise, audience, why you, comparable titles, chapter outline, sample chapter, platform. No source was opened for proposal formats; do not claim an industry standard beyond this.
- **Avoiding pseudo-history**: warning signs listed by Carroll and summarised at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory: treating myths as literal fact, cherry-picked documents, "could have happened" treated as "did happen", hidden-conspiracy suppression, a mission instead of a question. Examples cited there include von Daniken, Sitchin, Lemuria and The Holy Blood and the Holy Grail. Rule for Sol: present esoteric traditions as **what people believed and practised**, not as proven history; label legends as legends. [moderate]

## 4. Audience psychology and ethical persuasion
- **Cialdini's principles**: reciprocation, commitment and consistency, social proof, liking, authority, scarcity, plus unity added later (book first published 1984, Influence: Science and Practice 1985). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Influence:_Science_and_Practice (the page itself carries "citation needed" on the empirical-support claim). Evidence is **uneven by principle** [moderate for some, weak/contested for others]. One direct replication of the 1975 door-in-the-face finding abroad found similarly high compliance and supports reciprocity norms: https://fis.leuphana.de/en/publications/does-social-psychology-persist-over-half-a-century-a-direct-repli/ [moderate, single replication]. Do not cite specific percentages from the classic studies. For Aion Mind: ethical uses are honest social proof (real numbers), real scarcity (a live event with a date), and authority only with a verifiable source.
- **Narrative transportation**: the absorption into a story; the van Laer et al. 2014 meta-analysis of 132 effect sizes from 76 articles proposed an extended model (Journal of Consumer Research 40(5)). Source: https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/6755/. The press summary says realistic stories with identifiable characters and easy imagery transport most (https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/611188 appeared in search results only and was not opened). [moderate] Transportation lowers counter-arguing, so it carries an **ethical duty**: never use it to deliver false or health-affecting claims.
- **Nudges, effect sizes**: Mertens et al. 2022 (PNAS) reported an average Cohen's d of 0.43. Szaszi et al. say bias-adjusted estimates fall between d = 0.08 (severe bias) and 0.31 (moderate bias) and that effects vary widely, apart from a few exceptions such as defaults. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9351519/. Maier et al. 2022 argued with robust Bayesian methods that no evidence remains after adjusting for publication bias (title: "No evidence for nudging after adjusting for publication bias"; the letter itself could not be opened, so this rests on the title and search summaries). [weak/contested] Rule: assume any small nudge (button wording, default on a link) gives a small, uncertain effect; measure it yourself.
- **Dark patterns to avoid** (Brignull's term, 2010): bait-and-switch, drip pricing, confirmshaming, misdirection, privacy Zuckering, roach motel; regulators have fined companies for them. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_pattern. Applied to Sol: no fake countdowns, no pre-ticked boxes, no guilt-wording on "no thanks", no hard-to-cancel offers, no hidden sponsorship. [strong that they are regulated; harm-to-trust is craft convention]
- **Transparency rules**: disclose paid promotion; say "this is a psychology demonstration"; no therapy or health promises.
- **Platform policy checks before publishing** (re-read the pages each time; policies change):
  - Misleading metadata: titles, thumbnails and descriptions that lure viewers to content that does not deliver are prohibited; strikes and termination possible. https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973
  - Medical misinformation: content contradicting local health authority guidance on prevention or treatment is prohibited; educational or documentary context can be an exception. https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13813322 Never suggest an esoteric practice replaces treatment.
  - Advertiser-friendly: sensitive topics (self-harm, tragedies, shocking content) may limit ads; educational and documentary context counts; self-certify in Studio. https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278

## 5. Market and audience research, and statistics for small samples
- **Search demand**: YouTube search suggest and Google Trends. Trends shows a 0-100 index relative to share of searches, not absolute volume; low-volume terms read 0; small noise is added; a spike shows interest, not popularity. Source: https://support.google.com/trends/answer/4365533 [moderate]. Use it to compare topics against each other, never to estimate views.
- **Competitor analysis ethics**: study public titles, structure, pacing and comments; extract the pattern, never copy script, art or thumbnail. No scraping of private data. [craft convention]
- **Audience interviews**: 5-8 short conversations with real viewers; ask what they were doing when they clicked and what they felt at the end; do not ask them to predict. [craft convention]
- **Reading Studio metrics** (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415): flat retention line = watching to the end; gradual decline is normal; spikes = rewatched or shared (or unclear parts); dips = skipping or leaving; average view duration = minutes watched among those who stayed; key moments need at least 60 s and 100 views. CTR and impressions come from Studio analytics; do not apply rules of thumb for "good" values without the channel's own baseline.
- **Thumbnail/title A/B**: Studio "Test & compare" tests up to 3 titles and/or thumbnails, desktop only, not for Shorts or Premieres; winner is chosen by **watch time share**, not CTR; results are Winner, Performed Same or Inconclusive; the test usually ends within two weeks; editing the video mid-test stops it. Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400 and https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714 [moderate, platform documentation]. Test ONE change per test.
- **Confidence intervals**: a range for an unknown value; larger samples give narrower ranges; it does not mean "95% chance this interval contains the truth". Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confidence_interval [strong].
- **Why 3 videos prove nothing**: three results give an interval so wide that almost any story fits; differences between videos include topic, date, season, thumbnail and luck. [strong, statistical principle]
- **Regression to the mean**: an extreme result is usually followed by a more average one; Kahneman showed flight instructors misattributed this to their feedback. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regression_toward_the_mean [strong]. After a record video, expect the next to be lower even if nothing changed.
- **Multiple comparisons**: with m tests at 0.05, chance of at least one false positive is 1-(0.95)^m (6 tests: about 26.5%); Bonferroni divides alpha by m but is conservative. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_comparisons_problem [strong]. Do not test 10 metrics and report the one that "won".

## 6. Forecasting with explicit uncertainty
- Every forecast = **range (low / central / high), probability or confidence, and base rate** (e.g., median result of the last N comparable videos). Without a base rate, say "no base rate available".
- **Brier score** for yes/no forecasts: mean of (forecast probability minus outcome 0/1) squared; 0 is perfect, lower is better, always saying 50% scores 0.25. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brier_score [strong].
- Calibration: of all forecasts at 70%, about 7 in 10 should come true. The Good Judgment Project (Tetlock) scored forecasters with Brier scores and selected well-calibrated amateurs. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superforecasting [moderate; page is thin on method].
- Wording template: "Expected 800-3,000 views in 28 days (80% interval); base rate: median 1,100 across the last 6 long videos; confidence low because n=6." Never write "this will go viral".

## 7. Learning-loop protocol (after each publication)
1. **Before publishing**: write hypothesis, forecast (range, confidence, base rate) and decision rule.
2. **At fixed checkpoints** (e.g., 48 h, 7 d, 28 d): record impressions, CTR, views, average view duration, retention dips and spikes with timestamps, subscribers gained, comments themes.
3. **Entry fields**: video id, topic, format, title, thumbnail variant, hook line, structure used (ABT, circle, kishotenketsu), length, words per minute, forecast, outcome, forecast error, Brier score if yes/no, probable causes (max 3, labelled hypothesis), lesson, next experiment, policy flags.
4. **Lesson rule**: a lesson is "provisional" until supported by at least 3 independent comparable cases AND explained by a mechanism; label it [weak/contested] until then.
5. **Quarterly review**: recompute calibration and Brier score; retire lessons that failed to repeat.

## Claims to avoid
- "The hero's journey is universal" or "all stories follow three acts".
- "Studies show nudges/Cialdini tricks always work" or any invented percentages.
- "This thumbnail/title is better" from one test with an Inconclusive or Performed Same label.
- "Our video went viral because of X" from a single video.
- Any bare prediction (views, revenue, growth) with no range, base rate and confidence.
- Treating myths, legends or esoteric claims as proven history; "ancient secret suppressed".
- Any health, therapy or medical claim; any claim to read minds or foretell lives.
- Misleading titles or thumbnails, fake urgency, hidden sponsorship.
- Quoting Studio metric thresholds as "good" without the channel's own baseline.
