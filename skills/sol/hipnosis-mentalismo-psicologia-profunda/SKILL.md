# Hypnosis, mentalism and depth psychology (Sol)

## How to use this skill
1. Never claim to control, program or read minds. Hypnosis is cooperative; mentalism is theatre. Say so on screen when relevant.
2. Never make therapy, diagnosis or cure claims. Content is "education and imagination", not treatment. No "this will heal your anxiety/pain/trauma".
3. Tag every factual claim by evidence level in the research notes; in scripts, use hedges that match the tag ("research suggests", "debated", "a poetic idea").
4. Separate the three registers aloud: science (what studies show), tradition (what Freud/Jung proposed), and story (metaphor, myth, performance). Never let a story-register line pose as a finding.
5. When unsure whether a line is safe, cut it or reframe it as the character's belief, not the channel's.

## 1. Clinical hypnosis
**Solid**
- Division 30 (Society of Psychological Hypnosis, APA) wording as I could confirm through search results (not the official page, which returned 404): hypnosis is a state of consciousness with focused attention, reduced peripheral awareness and enhanced capacity to respond to suggestion; an induction is the procedure; hypnotherapy is hypnosis used to treat a condition. [strong as a definition; the wording itself is criticised, see below] https://read.qxmd.com/read/25365125/advancing-research-and-practice-the-revised-apa-division-30-definition-of-hypnosis
- Susceptibility: roughly 10% of people sit at each extreme, most in the middle; scores are fairly stable across life. The Stanford Scale (Weitzenhoffer and Hilgard, 1959) uses 12 suggestion tests after an eye-fixation induction. The "10-15% highly susceptible" figure is a rough range; sources disagree on the exact distribution. [moderate] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypnosis
- IBS: a meta-analysis of 7 randomized trials (374 adults, refractory IBS) found a borderline short-term benefit on abdominal pain at 3 months (P = 0.050), high heterogeneity, no reliable 1-year evidence, and no blinding possible. [moderate-weak] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4015203/
- Pain: a 2014 American Psychologist review (Jensen and Patterson) reports growing trial evidence that hypnosis reduces chronic pain with large individual differences; an earlier 2006 review found self-hypnosis performed about the same as progressive relaxation or autogenic training and had not been tested against a credible placebo. [moderate] I saw these only as abstracts in search results (PMC page blocked), so cite cautiously.
- Reviews of meta-analyses (Häuser et al. 2016) and a 2024 gastroenterology network meta-analysis also include hypnosis for IBS; I saw only snippets. [moderate]

**Contested**
- Whether hypnosis is a special "trance" state. State theories say yes; nonstate (sociocognitive) theories explain it by expectancy, role, motivation and strategy; some argue any trance accounts for a small share of effects. Kihlstrom has argued the two views are complementary. [weak/contested] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypnosis ; https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/social-cognitive-theories-of-hypnosis/
- Hilgard's neodissociation: attention splits so part of the mind registers pain while another does not (cold-water experiment: no reported pain, yet most subjects raised a finger on request). Influential, not settled. [moderate]
- Anxiety: I did not open a meta-analysis on hypnosis for anxiety; do not state an effect size. [speculative until verified]
- Spiegel's work is not verified here; do not attribute specific claims to him.

**What hypnosis is NOT**
- Not mind control. Not reliable memory retrieval: it can help create false memories and raise confidence in them; the APA and AMA caution that real and false memories cannot be told apart without corroboration. [strong] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypnosis
- Freud abandoned hypnosis for free association because results were inconsistent. [moderate] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigmund_Freud

**Use in content**
- Say "hypnosis research suggests some people get short-term relief from some conditions; ask a licensed clinician."
- Do not promise results from a video. Never imply the viewer is "highly susceptible".
- Do not offer age regression, "recover lost memories", or "find the root of your trauma" scripts.

## 2. Self-hypnosis and auto-suggestion
**Solid**
- Coué (1857-1926): repeat "Every day, in every way, I'm getting better and better" about twenty times at waking and bedtime; he said conscious willpower undermines the process. His evidence was anecdotal; critics blamed charisma, spontaneous remission and misdiagnosis, and warned of delayed treatment. [weak/contested] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89mile_Cou%C3%A9
- Schultz's autogenic training (published 1932) is the structured descendant: six exercises (heaviness, warmth, calm heartbeat, breathing, abdominal warmth, cool forehead), learned stepwise. A 2002 meta-analysis (Stetter and Kupper, 60 studies) reported benefits similar to other recommended relaxation therapies. [moderate; page itself flagged for weak referencing] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autogenic_training
- Direct research on "autosuggestion" per se is very limited; relaxation, imagery and autogenic training have the better data. [moderate] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autosuggestion

**Contested**
- Coué-style claims of organic cure. [weak/contested]
- Claims that autogenic training "rebalances the nervous system" (rests on a weak source). [speculative]

**Use in content**
- Offer short, generic relaxation or imagery as "an exercise some people find calming", seated, eyes optional, not while driving or operating machinery.
- Present Coué as history and a cautionary tale about positive-thinking overreach.
- Autogenic training: a sceptic's note that it is for qualified guidance; not for children under 5 or people with uncontrollable symptoms.

## 3. Suggestion, placebo, nocebo, expectation
**Solid**
- Nocebo: negative expectation worsens outcomes or produces side effects; verbal suggestion can increase pain sensitivity. A 2022 meta-analysis attributed most post-COVID-vaccine adverse effects (72% after dose one) to nocebo responses; ethics tension: informed consent vs harmful framing. [moderate-strong] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nocebo
- Open-label placebo: Kaptchuk et al. 2010 (PLoS ONE), 80 IBS patients, 3 weeks, honestly labelled placebo pills beat no treatment on global improvement; small, short, self-selected sample. [moderate-weak, needs replication] https://www.healthday.com/healthpro-news/general-health/placebos-without-deception-still-work-in-ibs-647799.html ; https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120378-9982-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b
- Barnum/Forer: 39 students rated an identical generic description about 4.3/5; acceptance rises with vague wording, positive content, belief it is personal, and trust in the source. [strong] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnum_effect

**Contested**
- How large placebo effects are outside subjective symptoms (pain, nausea). [moderate]
- Priming effects on behaviour (weak replication; see companion guide). [weak/contested]

**Use in content**
- Storytelling that evokes expectancy (curiosity, warmth) is legitimate; do not seed negative expectations about the viewer's health ("you will feel worse if...").
- Never say "your body will heal itself" or imply stopping medication.

## 4. Psychoanalysis and depth psychology
**Freud.** Dreams as disguised wish-fulfilment via dream-work; unconscious/preconscious/conscious model; repression; free association replaced hypnosis. [Dream theory: weak/contested; "unconscious processes exist": strong] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigmund_Freud
**Jung.** Archetypes (innate patterns), collective unconscious (term from 1916), individuation (lifelong integration of persona, shadow, anima/animus toward the Self), synchronicity (meaningful coincidence without causation, with a famous scarab anecdote), active imagination (from about 1913-16). Jung himself saw the unconscious as a working hypothesis not directly testable; synchronicity's statistical tests were criticised; the MBTI is only loosely based on his types. [weak/contested; useful as symbol language] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytical_psychology
**The Red Book (Liber Novus).** Hand-lettered, illustrated, written about 1914-1930 from his Black Book notes, published only in October 2009 (facsimile, W. W. Norton); he called the period the most important of his life. [strong as history] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Red_Book_(Jung)
**Adler.** Inferiority feelings, striving for mastery, social interest, birth order (no empirical support offered by him; used now as a lens on subjective family experience). Influenced humanistic and cognitive therapists. [weak for birth order; moderate as influence] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Adler
**Lacan.** Presented as extending Freud; expelled from the International Psychoanalytical Association over unconventional technique. Only that is verified here; do not summarise his theory. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychoanalysis

**Solid / what survives**
- Cognitive unconscious (Kihlstrom's term, also "adaptive" or "implicit"): much processing happens outside awareness; implicit memory is well studied. But it is experimental, mostly without repression or symbolic disguise. [strong] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconscious_mind
- Psychodynamic therapy: small but significant benefits in a 2020 meta-analysis of long-term therapy; a 2012 review found a modest effect (0.33) and recovery no better than controls; often similar to active comparators. [moderate] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychoanalysis

**Contested**
- Popper: core psychoanalytic assumptions are not refutable, so some call it pseudoscience. [weak/contested]
- Oedipus complex, dream symbolism, collective unconscious, synchronicity as causal or paranormal. [weak/contested to speculative]

**Use in content**
- Frame Freud and Jung as maps and metaphors that shaped culture, not as verified anatomy of the mind.
- Say "Jung proposed", not "science shows". Archetypes are a storytelling vocabulary.
- Active imagination: describe as Jung's historical practice; do not instruct viewers in it for emotional "processing".

## 5. Mentalism and the psychology of deception
**Solid**
- Cold reading: probes, guesses and feedback; the subject supplies most information; hits are stressed, misses forgotten. Techniques: shotgunning, Barnum statements, warm reading, rainbow ruse (a trait plus its opposite). Explained by the Barnum effect and confirmation bias. [strong] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_reading
- Forcing: steering a "free" choice. Physical forces use sleight; the verbal one is equivocation (magician's choice), where every option leads to the same result; repetition weakens it. [moderate; craft knowledge] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forcing_(magic)
- Misdirection: look-away, reframing a trivial detail as important, props; Tommy Wonder: direct attention toward, rather than away. [moderate; craft knowledge] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misdirection_(magic)

**Contested / unverified**
- Magician-neuroscience (Macknik, Martinez-Conde; the Nature Reviews Neuroscience article) is cited in the companion guide; I could not open it (redirect). Treat as [moderate], unverified here.
- Claims that "body language reveals lies" or "NLP rapport programs people". [weak/contested]

**Ethics: honest showmanship**
- Performers such as Derren Brown and Ian Rowland have openly admitted to using cold reading; critics condemn readers who exploit the grieving. Framing as a performance, naming the methods when asked, and never charging vulnerable people for "messages from the dead" is the line. Never claim real psychic ability. [moderate] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_reading

**Use in content**
- Demonstrate a Barnum reading, then reveal why it works. This turns the trick into media literacy.
- Do not target named individuals with "readings" or implied diagnoses.

## 6. Use in content: ethical trance-like language, imagery and absorption
Allowed:
- Slow pacing, second-person imagery ("imagine a door"), pauses and soft music for narrative absorption, framed as a story or guided imagination, never "hypnosis session".
- Open-loop curiosity, nested stories and rhythm (see companion guide).
Required:
- Opening line or description disclaimer: "Educational and entertainment content. Not medical advice, therapy or treatment. Do not watch while driving or operating machinery. Stop if you feel uncomfortable."
- Pin the same disclaimer in book front matter and audio descriptions.
Prohibited:
- No dissociation, depersonalisation, "go deeper and deeper" commands, or counted inductions in driving, working or exercise contexts; no "you will obey".
- No suggestions about specific symptoms, weight, addiction, sleep disorders, trauma, abuse memories, or stopping treatment.
- No post-hypnotic triggers ("whenever you hear X you will...") and no unconsolidated "emergence" gaps: if you use relaxation, close with a clear re-orientation ("open your eyes, you are here, today's date is...").
- Contraindicated audiences: people with psychosis, active dissociative or trauma disorders, or uncontrolled seizure conditions (clinical caution; I did not verify a formal list, so advise a professional rather than listing diagnoses on screen).
Platform-safe wording: use "guided imagination", "relaxation story", "bedtime narration", "mental theatre", "psychology of suggestion". Avoid "cure", "heal", "rewire your subconscious", "manifest", "guaranteed", "clinically proven", "hypnotherapy" (unless licensed), and medical claims; YouTube and Meta restrict health claims. [no source opened; apply conservatively]

## Claims to avoid
- "Hypnosis can recover repressed memories" or "reveal past lives".
- "Hypnosis/Coué/autosuggestion cures disease."
- "10-15% of you can be hypnotised by this video."
- "Science has proven the collective unconscious/archetypes/synchronicity."
- "Freud proved dreams are repressed wishes."
- "I can read your mind / predict your future."
- "Your subconscious controls 95% of your life" (no source opened; unsupported statistic).
- "Open-label placebo is a proven treatment."
- Anything that tells viewers to stop treatment or replace professional care.
